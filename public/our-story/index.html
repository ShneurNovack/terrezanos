A family table on Rockefeller Lane

How a small trattoria near the studios became Midtown's favorite plate of pasta, and stayed that way through one very complicated Saturday night.

"If the sauce is not right, it does not leave the kitchen. Nothing leaves the kitchen. Everything comes from the kitchen."Chef Luigi Marinara

Terrezano's opened its doors on a Saturday night in late September, the kind of night when the whole city seems to be out looking for something live. Chef Luigi Marinara brought the recipes his family has cooked for generations: a Sunday gravy that simmers for six hours, a vodka sauce finished with real Calabrian chili, and fettuccine cut by hand to the width of his thumb.

We are a small room with checked tablecloths, Chianti bottles holding their candles, and a staff that will remember your order the second time you visit. Couples get engaged here. Families argue happily about whose turn it is to pay. Regulars tell us our pasta is the best they have ever had, and we believe them, because we watched Luigi make it.

Every dish on our menu comes out of one kitchen, ours, through the swinging door at the back of the dining room. You are always welcome to peek. That door has nothing to hide.

Our first night

September 30. We remember it hour by hour.

  1. 5:00 pm
    Doors open

    Checked tablecloths pressed, candles lit, the Sunday gravy six hours in. Luigi tastes it twice and nods.

  2. 7:30 pm
    The first regulars arrive

    A couple at table six orders the spaghetti al pomodoro. She tells the table she is half Italian and knows pasta. He agrees with everything she says.

  3. 11:30 pm
    A very friendly man in a suit appears

    He has a microphone and an announcement about where the food came from. We do not remember what he said. We have chosen not to.

  4. 11:31 pm
    Things get loud

    Table six has questions. Mark is asked, several times, to back somebody up. Luigi stays in the kitchen, where he has always been.

  5. Every night since
    Real pasta, real kitchen

    Rolled every afternoon, cooked to order, served by people who will look you in the eye and tell you exactly where it came from. Here.

What we promise

Everything from scratch
Pasta, sauces, breadsticks, dolci and limoncello are made in our kitchen.
Ingredients we can name
San Marzano tomatoes, Parmigiano-Reggiano aged 24 months, olive oil from a family press in Puglia.
No surprises
Nobody will jump out with a microphone. The only reveal at Terrezano's is dessert.

Come see for yourself

The kitchen door swings both ways. Bring someone you want to impress.
